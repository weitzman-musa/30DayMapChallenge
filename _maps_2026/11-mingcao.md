**Day 11 (Senses: Sound)**: Where Sounds Ripple explores how the character “水” (water) is pronounced at Yue-related survey locations in Academia Sinica Xiaoxuetang's phonology collection. Water patterns draw on the visual language of traditional East Asian water art, balancing approximate geography with an artistic composition.

![Where Sounds Ripple: water-pattern clusters across Yue-related survey locations, with color showing initials and four textures showing selected finals]({{ '/assets/thumbnails/2026/11-mingcao.png' | relative_url }})

Color represents the recorded initial consonant. Four water-pattern types represent distinct original finals: Distributed Currents (ui), Layered Water Field (œy), Curling Waves (øy), and Crested Waves (oi). Eight other final categories remain separate, with in-frame observations shown as initial-colored anchors. Similar-looking transcriptions are not merged. Tone is retained in the data but is not encoded in the water patterns.

The animation highlights the four pattern types in turn:

![Where Sounds Ripple: looping animation highlighting the four water-pattern categories while geography, labels, and observation anchors stay fixed]({{ '/assets/thumbnails/2026/11-mingcao.gif' | relative_url }})

The project retains 79 original records: 74 have approximate coordinates, 73 fall within the main frame, Bangkok lies outside it, and five remain unlocated. Within the frame, 48 water clusters represent 60 records; 13 others are anchor-only. Shared clusters combine nearby records only when their original initial and final match exactly. Locations may be town or county centers; textures are adjusted for readability. Their size and coverage do not represent dialect boundaries, population, phonetic distance, or actual sound waves. Blank space does not establish absence of a pronunciation.

**Data sources:**

- [Academia Sinica Xiaoxuetang phonology downloads](https://xiaoxue.iis.sinica.edu.tw/ccrdata/): retained Yue survey workbooks and saved survey-location links. Readings use the archived package consistently; source notation may vary, and file timestamps are not fieldwork dates.
- Geographic context: Natural Earth and [NOAA ETOPO 2022](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/etopo-global-relief-model), with generalized coastlines and bathymetry.
