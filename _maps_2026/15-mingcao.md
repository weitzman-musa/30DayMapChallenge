**Day 15 (Inside out)**: Philadelphia: A Gazetteer of Places draws on traditional Chinese local gazetteer maps to reveal what a city contains. The Schuylkill and Delaware Rivers frame selected landmarks, bridges, major streets, and groups of rowhouses. Streets connect places, while important buildings appear at different scales and viewpoints. Relative relationships remain recognizable even as distances and positions are simplified.

This pictorial interpretation turns Philadelphia inside out through the buildings, places, and connections that give it character. It is not a geographically precise map. The broken vegetation edge suggests William Penn's planned river-to-river core, rather than a city wall or municipal boundary.

**Technical implementation and sources:**

- Python, NumPy, Shapely, Matplotlib, and SVG; selectively drawn architectural symbols and spline-based river ribbons.

- Visual references: the 1613 Fuzhou gazetteer city map, compiled by Yu Zheng and Lin Cai, with digitization attributed to the National Archives of Japan; and the 1840 Jinan gazetteer city map. Both historical works and their reproductions are recorded as public domain in the project's reference notes.
